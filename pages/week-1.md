### Part 2 — RFI Lab

Each pair of teams takes one side of a real coordination relationship:

- **GC team:** designs a new UW Information School building at the program-brief level (site, program spaces, structural systems, major divisions of work, key interfaces between trades). The GC team then **publishes a formal RFI** to its paired subcontractor team: written in proper RFI format (RFI number, subject, reference documents, question, options considered, deadline for response, required format).
- **Subcontractor team:** receives the RFI and produces an **RFI response package** — a direct answer, assumptions made, any cost or schedule implications, and supporting references. Responses must arrive by the RFI's stated deadline.

**Rules of the lab:**
- The GC team may issue **one addendum** if the subcontractor's response is ambiguous or non-responsive (this is part of the exercise).
- Both teams log every communication: timestamps, questions, clarifications. The communication log is a deliverable.

**Debrief (end of class):** map the exercise onto real GC/sub coordination — ambiguity in contract documents, latency, who bears the risk of a late or wrong answer, how an AI agent could have helped (or hurt) at each step. Each team drafts the kernel of its quarter-long **thesis**: *where does information flow break down in construction, and how would you test that?*
